# Scoring

Surround uses **area scoring**. When a game ends, each player's score is:

> **their living stones on the board + the empty points only their stones
> surround**

and White adds [komi](#komi). The higher score wins.

## What counts

- **Living stones.** Every stone of yours on the board, except the ones both
  players [agreed are dead](#dead-stones).
- **Your area.** An empty region (a connected set of empty points) counts
  for you if every stone along its edge is yours. The eyes inside your groups
  count, as does any territory you've walled off.
- **Neutral points.** A region whose edge touches stones of both colors
  counts for nobody. That covers **dame**, the leftover points between the
  two sides, and the **shared liberties** of a seki, where neither side can
  approach the other without being captured.
- **Captures add nothing.** Captured stones are recorded, and shown on the
  kifu, but they aren't added to the score. Under area scoring a capture pays
  off through the board instead: the stones are gone and their points
  become yours.

This is not the Japanese territory scoring many players learn first, which
counts captures plus surrounded empty points. The two usually agree on the
winner, but they can differ by a point.

## Dead stones

Some stones are still on the board at the end but can't escape capture. Both
players agree which stones are **dead**, and they're scored as if they'd been
captured: they count for nobody, and their points join the area of whoever
surrounds them.

A dead-stone proposal has to mark **whole groups**: a dead stone's
same-colored neighbors must be dead too. How players agree, and what happens
when they don't, is under [How a game ends](/gameplay/ending#scoring-a-game).

Black wins by 1: **B+1**. Komi nearly closed the gap.

The dead white stone counts for no one, and its point is part of Black's
area, since only black stones surround it. The point at the bottom touches
both colors, so it's neutral. Every point on the board is accounted for:
Black's 44, White's 36 before komi, and 1 neutral point make 81.

### Why playing on costs nothing

Under area scoring, playing a stone inside your own area doesn't change your
score: the point stops counting as area and starts counting as a stone.
Capturing a dead stone doesn't change it either: the stone was already
counted for no one, and its point goes back into your area once it's gone.

That's why a disagreement about dead stones can always be settled by playing
it out. Suppose the players above disagreed and played on. If Black never
captured the white stone, it would count as **alive** at the end, and the
empty points around it would touch both colors and turn neutral. Black would
lose all of its area, and the game. Capturing it first costs Black nothing.

## Komi

Black moves first, which is an advantage. **Komi** is a number of points
added to White's score to make up for it. It's fixed in each game's terms in
half-point steps.

| Board | Komi in rated games |
| --- | --: |
| 9×9 | 7 |
| 13×13 | 7.5 |
| 19×19 | 7.5 |

These are the matchmaker's current settings; the
[policy](/ranking/trust#the-policy) fixes the komi each rated board size may
use. Casual games set their own komi.

### Draws

With a half-point komi a draw is impossible. With a whole-number komi, such
as 7 on 9×9, the scores can tie, and the game is a **draw**. A draw counts
as half a win in the [rating update](/ranking/model#update), and has no kifu.

## Where scoring is checked

Scores are computed, never claimed. When a game ends by agreement, the
score is worked out from the final board and the agreed dead stones, and
when the game reaches the chain, Cairo computes it again from the same
inputs. A proof checks the arithmetic; it never decides which stones are
dead. The players do.
