# Playing a game

Surround plays Go on a **9×9, 13×13 or 19×19** board. Two players take turns
placing stones on the intersections, black first. Stones never move once
placed; they leave the board only when they're captured. The game is won by
controlling more of the board ([Scoring](/gameplay/scoring)).

## Who plays black

Seat 0 plays black and moves first; seat 1 plays white and receives
[komi](/gameplay/scoring#komi). In a rated game the matchmaker's ticket names
who sits where: at an open table the host plays black. Games are even:
Surround has no handicap stones.

## A turn

On your turn you do one of three things:

- **Play** a stone on an empty intersection, if the move is legal (below).
- **Pass.** Passing is always legal. Two passes in a row end play and start
  [scoring](/gameplay/ending#scoring-a-game).
- **Resign**, which ends the game at once. You can resign at any time, even
  on your opponent's turn.

## Liberties and capture

The empty intersections next to a stone (up, down, left and right, not
diagonally) are its **liberties**. Stones of one color that touch along the
lines form a **group** and share their liberties.

A group whose last liberty is filled by the opponent is **captured**: all its
stones come off the board.

Captured stones are counted and shown on the game record, but under
Surround's area scoring they add nothing to the score by themselves: what
counts is the board at the end.

## Suicide

You may not play a stone that leaves your own group with no liberties.

**Captures come first.** If your stone takes the last liberty of an opposing
group, that group is removed before your own liberties are checked. A move
that captures is therefore never suicide.

## Ko and positional superko

In the last diagram, Black would like to play straight back into the
captured point, capturing the white stone that just took. That recreates the
position from one move earlier, and the two players could trade captures
forever. This shape is a **ko**.

Surround uses **positional superko**: **no move may recreate any whole-board
position that has occurred before in the game.** Black has to play somewhere
else first (a "ko threat"), and can retake on a later turn once the position
has changed.

Superko covers every earlier position, not only the one just before, which
also rules out longer cycles. Passes are exempt: a pass doesn't change the
board, so it's always legal. The full position history is kept for the whole
game, including across a [scoring resumption](/gameplay/ending#disagreeing).

## Time

Casual games are untimed. Ranked games are timed by a referee clock, either
60 seconds a move or Japanese byo-yomi; running out loses the game. See
[Ranked clocks](/settlement/clocks).

## How moves are checked

Every move is signed by the player who makes it, and both players' clients
check each move against these rules before accepting it. When a game
reaches the chain, Cairo replays every move under the same rules, superko
included. See [Offchain play](/settlement/offchain-play).
