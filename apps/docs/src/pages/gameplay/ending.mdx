# How a game ends

A game ends in one of six ways:

| Ending | How | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Agreement** | Both players pass, then agree which stones are dead. | Scored: `B+3.5`, `W+7` |
| **Played out** | After the game's one resumption, both players pass again. | Scored, every stone alive |
| **Move limit** | The game reaches its move limit. | Scored, every stone alive |
| **Resignation** | A player resigns. | `B+R`, `W+R` |
| **Time** | A player's clock runs out (ranked games). | `B+T`, `W+T` |
| **Forfeit** | A player stops responding during forced onchain play. | `B+F`, `W+F` |

The notation is SGF's, and it's what a game's [kifu](/kifu) shows. A scored
game can also be a [draw](/gameplay/scoring#draws).

## Scoring a game

Play stops after **two passes in a row**. The game moves to scoring, a
short, turn-based exchange:

1. **Propose.** The player who passed first proposes which stones are dead:
   a complete set, whole groups only. That player may instead resume play.
2. **Accept or resume.** The other player accepts the proposal, which ends
   the game with the [score](/gameplay/scoring) it gives, or resumes play.

The exchange is strictly one at a time, so there is always exactly one
player due to act, which is what lets a [dispute](/settlement/disputes)
decide who has stalled. The app lets both players mark groups on their own
boards before anything is signed, so the proposal usually just confirms what
both already see.

The proposal must mark whole groups: a dead stone's neighbors of its own
color must be dead too. A proposal that splits a group is refused.

### Disagreeing

**Resuming** returns to play with the board, captures and the full position
history kept, so [superko](/gameplay/playing#ko-and-positional-superko) still
applies to every position so far. The proposal is cleared.

- If the proposer resumes, the proposer moves next.
- If the responder resumes, play returns to the player who was due after
  the passes, which is again the proposer.

A game can resume **only once**. After that, two passes end it immediately,
**scored with every stone on the board alive**. There's no second proposal.

This is how disagreements end. Under area scoring,
[capturing a dead stone costs nothing](/gameplay/scoring#why-playing-on-costs-nothing),
so whoever thinks a group is dead can simply capture it, and whoever thinks
it's alive can defend it. The board settles the question, and a losing
player can't stall forever with pass, pass, resume.

:::tip
After a resumption, capture every dead stone of your opponent's that sits in
your area before you pass. The game will be scored exactly as the board
stands.
:::

## Move limits

Two limits bound every game. A move here is a stone or a pass.

| Board | Moves in all | Moves after the resumption |
| --- | --: | --: |
| 9×9 | 243 | 162 |
| 13×13 | 507 | 338 |
| 19×19 | 1,083 | 722 |

That's three times the board's points in all, and twice its points after the
resumption: enough to capture every dead stone and fill every point. A game
that reaches either limit ends at once, scored like a played-out game. Real
games end long before.

## Resignation

Either player can resign at any time, including on the opponent's turn and
during scoring. If the game has already reached the chain, a player can also
resign from their wallet with `resign_channel`.

## Time

In a ranked game, a player who runs out of time loses. The referee appends a
**flag** to the game, and the result is `B+T` or `W+T`. Scoring is timed like
play: the proposal and the response each take a turn of the clock. See
[Ranked clocks](/settlement/clocks).

## Forfeit

If a player stops responding, the opponent can take the game to the chain
and force it to continue there, one turn at a time, each with a deadline. A
player who misses a deadline **forfeits**: `B+F` or `W+F`. See
[Disputes](/settlement/disputes#forced-play).

## Short games

A game shorter than 20 steps that ends offchain doesn't count for
[ratings](/ranking/rated-games#when-a-game-counts), as aborted games don't on
other servers. A resignation or forfeit decided onchain still counts, for
the loser only.

## Playing an AI anchor

The AI anchors follow the same rules. At scoring they propose the stones
KataGo judges dead, accept your proposal if it gives the same winner as
theirs, and otherwise resume. After a resumption they play at full strength
and capture what's dead. They never resign. See
[AI anchors](/ai-anchors#how-they-play).
