# AI anchors

Surround's five AI opponents are **anchors**: accounts whose ratings the
owner pins in `SurroundRatings`. Each plays at a fixed strength, with a
fixed rating to match, so the rank a newcomer earns against them means
something. Their games are rated like any other, but they rate only the
human.

## How they play

The anchors run on [KataGo](https://github.com/lightvector/KataGo)'s analysis
engine with its **human SL model** (`b18c384nbt-humanv0`, KataGo 1.15+). That
model was trained on human games and predicts the move a human of a given rank
would play. Each query names the anchor's rank profile, so one engine process
serves all five.

- **Moves are sampled, not searched.** For each move the anchor samples
  KataGo's human policy for its rank at its native temperature, skipping
  illegal moves (positional superko included). Turning down a superhuman
  search's visits doesn't make a believable 20k; sampling what a 20k would
  play does. A short search (64 visits) runs alongside to decide when to
  pass.
- **Passing.** The anchor passes only when the search recommends it. When its
  opponent has passed, it passes back if ending the game now, scored with
  KataGo's dead stones removed, gives up at most one point against playing on.
  Otherwise an engine keeps filling its own territory, which costs nothing
  under area scoring, and the game never ends.
- **Scoring.** After two passes, the anchor proposes the stones KataGo's
  ownership estimate marks dead: groups whose average ownership leans past
  0.8 toward the opponent, on KataGo's scale of −1 to 1. It accepts its opponent's proposal if that gives the same
  winner as its own, and resumes play otherwise.
- **After a resumption, full strength.** Once a game has been resumed, the
  stones on the board are what counts, so the anchor drops its rank profile
  and plays KataGo's best move, capturing what's dead.
- **Anchors don't resign.** They play every game to scoring or a flag.

## How their ratings work

An anchor's rating is its pin, not something it earns.

- **Fixed.** An anchor plays at its pinned μ with a fixed φ of 0.25. Its
  rating never ages, is never stored, and never shows "?".
- **One-sided.** Its games rate only its opponent, by the ordinary
  [update](/ranking/model#update).
- **Never against each other.** An anchor's ticket band is 0, and two anchors
  never play a rated game.
- **Rank, not peak.** An anchor never counts as a settled player, so games
  against anchors can move a human's rank but never their
  [peak](/ranking/model#peak). That bounds what a repeatable trick against
  the engine is worth.
- **Visible in the audit trail.** An anchor's side of `RatingUpdated` is
  marked `anchor` and shows its pin before and after; `replay.mjs` checks it
  against the owner's `set_anchor` events.

### Why pin an AI, but not humans

Freezing settled *human* players against newcomers was tested and failed
([evidence](/ranking/evidence#rule-changes)): a human's strength moves. An
engine at a fixed profile and visit count doesn't, so a fixed rating is the
right model for it.

A floating rating would be worse for an AI in two ways. Sybil accounts
starting at 6k could lose to the 20k anchor on purpose, inflating it for real
newcomers to farm. And an AI that has played thousands of games reaches the
minimum φ and becomes an anchor anyway, at whatever level newcomers' bands
dragged it to.

## Calibration

Anchors are pinned at their nominal ranks, but KataGo's human profiles
learned **Fox's** ranks, not OGS's, and high-dan imitation is approximate.
`replay.mjs` reports each anchor's points against established players next to
the points expected. An anchor that keeps scoring above expectation is
stronger than its pin, and the owner re-pins it with `set_anchor`. The
[rank offset](/ranking/ranks#drift) shifts anchors' shown ranks along with
everyone else's.

`set_anchor` accepts only an account that has never been rated, and once the
contract is sealed it waits 48 hours like any loosening change.
`remove_anchor` applies at once and voids the anchor's unrated games.

## The anchor daemon

One process plays all five anchors. For each, it:

1. **Offers session keys.** It offers the matchmaker fresh session keys for
   the anchor's next games, signed by the anchor's wallet, keeping each
   private key in its own store first. An anchor with no keys on offer is
   busy.
2. **Checks and signs terms.** It checks each pairing as any player's client
   does (the ticket, its board, komi and clock against the ticket's hashes,
   its own key at its seat, band 0, the keeper's referee) and signs the terms
   with the anchor's wallet.
3. **Plays through the keeper**, like any ranked game, on the referee's
   clock.

A player asks the matchmaker for an anchor by name and is offered a game at
once; it's theirs when their wallet signs the terms. The anchors' accounts
only sign. They never send transactions: the keeper opens and settles their
games, and the matchmaker rates them.

## Trust

Whoever holds an anchor's wallet key could throw its games to inflate a
friend's rank. That's the operator, who already holds the matchmaker key. A
new anchor waits 48 hours once the contract is sealed, the same rule as for
new matchmaker keys.

:::info[Not the practice lobby]
The web app's guest lobby uses the same engine for unrated practice and an
app-side placement estimate. Those games and estimates are separate from
`SurroundRatings` and never reach the chain.
:::
